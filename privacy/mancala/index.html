Mancala privacy policy

Effective date: November 21, 2026

This policy explains what information the iOS app Mancala (“the app”) accesses, collects, uses and shares. The app is made by Handyfox (“we”, “us”), the developer shown on its App Store page. Contact for privacy questions: kieulong1302@gmail.com.

In short

  • There's no account or sign-up. We don't ask for your name, email, contacts, photos or precise location.
  • What you do in the app is stored on your phone.
  • Google services built into the app (ads, analytics, crash reports, remote settings) receive device and usage information, described below.
  • Ads appear only when you tap to watch a video for an extra. The main features work without watching one.
  • Ads use your device's advertising identifier (IDFA) only if you allow tracking. The app works the same either way.

1. Permissions and data on your device

Mancala may ask for these permissions:

  • Tracking: Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt, shown once after your first finished game. Your answer decides whether the app may give your device's advertising identifier (IDFA) to Google's ad and analytics services, as described in section 2. The game works the same whether you allow it or not, and you can change your answer any time in Settings › Privacy & Security › Tracking.

The app also connects to the internet, which iOS allows without asking, to load the videos you choose to watch, to tell you when a video needs an internet connection, and for the Google services below.

Kept only on your device

  • The game in progress: rules and options, the moves played so far, whose turn it is, undos left, hints used and time played, so you can continue after closing the app.
  • Your record against the computer (wins, losses and draws for each rule and level).
  • Boards and seed sets you unlocked by watching a video, and the ones you use.
  • Your settings (sowing speed, seed counts, sound, vibration), your last game setup, and whether you finished the welcome screens and the tutorial.
  • Small records the app keeps for itself: features you unlocked for a limited time and when they expire, daily free-use counters, and when the app last asked you to rate it.

We don't receive this data. It stays in the app's private storage until you delete the app. If iCloud Backup is turned on, iOS includes it in your iCloud backup, and it's also part of any backup of your device that you make on a computer, so it can be restored on a new device. Apple handles iCloud backups under the Apple Privacy Policy.

2. Information collected by services in the app

The app uses the following services from Google. They collect information automatically when you use the app and send it over encrypted connections to Google, which processes it for us and, in some cases, for its own purposes. You can read how Google uses information from apps that use its services.

Google AdMob (rewarded video ads)

Shows a video when you choose to watch one in exchange for an extra. AdMob collects a device identifier (described below), IP address (from which it can estimate your approximate location), device and app information such as model, operating system version, language and app version, and how you interact with ads. It uses this to deliver and measure ads, limit how often you see the same ad, and prevent fraud. Personalized ads are shown only where you've agreed or where the law allows. We ask AdMob to limit ad content to ratings suitable for teens (T) or lower.

AdMob receives your device's advertising identifier (IDFA) only if you allow tracking (see App Tracking Transparency below). It may also use identifiers that are limited to this app or to apps from the same developer, and it may collect app performance data, such as launch time and hang rate, and crash logs from the ads SDK itself. Installs that come from ads can be measured with Apple's privacy-preserving SKAdNetwork, which doesn't use the IDFA.

Google User Messaging Platform (consent)

If you're in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, Switzerland or another region where the law requires it, the app shows Google's consent form before requesting ads, so you can choose how your data is used for advertising. The form stores your choice on your device. You can change it any time in the app under Settings › Privacy options; this item appears where the law requires it.

App Tracking Transparency

The app may ask, using Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt, whether it may track your activity across other companies' apps and websites. If you allow it, the app can share your device's advertising identifier (IDFA) with Google: AdMob can use it to show you more relevant ads and to measure them, and Google Analytics for Firebase receives it too. If you don't allow it, iOS doesn't give the app the IDFA. Either way, you can still watch videos for extras, and every feature works the same. You can change your choice any time in Settings › Privacy & Security › Tracking.

Google Analytics for Firebase

Helps us understand how the app is used so we can improve it. It collects an app instance ID, device information, app version, approximate location (country or region, based on IP address), and app usage such as when the app is opened, session length and screens viewed. The app also logs events of its own about how the app is used: which screens you open and in what order, the steps of the welcome screens and tutorial, which features you use (such as resuming a game, using a hint or an undo, or opening the game log), the content you pick, what you finish and how it went (for example, how long it took), settings you change, your answers to permission requests, links you open from the app, errors the app shows you, and whether a video offer was shown, accepted, declined, completed or failed, with the reason it failed (for example, no internet). These events don't include anything you type, your name, email or precise location.

It also collects the identifier for vendors (IDFV), which is the same for all apps from one developer on your device, and the advertising identifier (IDFA) only if you allow tracking.

Firebase Crashlytics

Sends a report when the app crashes or hits an error, so we can fix it. A report contains technical details such as the error trace, device model, operating system version, app version and a random installation identifier.

Firebase Remote Config

Lets us change settings without an app update, for example whether a feature asks you to watch a video. It uses a Firebase installation identifier and basic device and app information such as app version and language.

App Store rating prompt

Now and then the app may ask you to rate it with Apple's standard rating prompt. Your rating, and your review if you write one, go to Apple and appear on the App Store like any other rating or review. The app itself doesn't receive them. You can turn these prompts off in Settings › App Store › In-App Ratings & Reviews.

More details: Google Privacy Policy, Privacy and security in Firebase, Apple Privacy Policy.

3. How we use and share information

We use the information above only to run the app, show the videos you choose to watch, keep the app stable, and decide what to improve. We share it only with Google as the provider of the services above, and with authorities when the law requires it. We don't share it with any other company.

Where data protection laws such as the GDPR apply, we rely on your consent for personalized ads and for ad-related storage on your device where that's required. For analytics, crash reports, remote settings and fraud prevention, we rely on our legitimate interest in keeping the app working and improving it.

4. Your choices

  • Change your ad consent in Settings › Privacy options (where shown).
  • Allow or stop tracking for this app in Settings › Privacy & Security › Tracking. Turning off Allow Apps to Request to Track there denies tracking to every app.
  • Turn off any permission the app has in the iOS Settings app at any time.
  • Uninstall the app to stop all collection and remove the data stored on your device.

5. Retention and deletion

Data on your device stays until you delete the app. We keep user-level analytics data for no more than 14 months. Crash reports are kept for 90 days. Google may keep advertising data according to its own policies.

We don't know who you are, because the app has no accounts. If you'd like us to delete analytics or crash data linked to your device, or you have any other privacy request, email kieulong1302@gmail.com and we'll help.

6. Security

Google's services send data over encrypted connections. Data the app stores on your device is kept in the app's private storage, which other apps can't read.

7. Children

The app is not directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from them. If you believe a child has used the app and data was collected, contact us and we'll help delete it.

8. Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we'll update this page and the effective date at the top. If a change affects how your data is handled in a significant way, we'll also mention it in the app's release notes on the App Store.
